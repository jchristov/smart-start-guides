Inside the opening chapter

Sofia (София) is one of Europe’s oldest cities and, for many visitors, one of its most pleasant surprises. Roman streets lie beneath the metro station, a Byzantine-style cathedral glitters a few minutes from a Soviet-era park, and a mosque, a synagogue and two churches stand within sight of one another. Beyond the last tram stop rises Vitosha, a mountain that locals reach on a city bus and that turns the skyline into a daily reminder that the wilderness is close.

The city is compact, walkable and relaxed. Its centre can be crossed on foot in half an hour; its cafés are inexpensive by Western European standards; and its food, built on grilled meat, fresh salads, yoghurt, cheese pastries and fruit brandy, is honest and generous. Bulgaria is a full member of the Schengen Area (land borders since 2025) and has used the euro since 1 January 2026, which makes arrival simpler for most European visitors.

SMART START TIP

Sofia rewards a relaxed pace. Plan one big sight in the morning, one in the afternoon, and keep the evening for a long dinner on a terrace. The best discoveries here are often a courtyard, a church or a bookstall found while walking between the sights.
