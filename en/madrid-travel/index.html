Inside the opening chapter

Madrid is the city that Spain built for itself. It has no beach, no great river and no medieval skyline to set it apart from the postcards. What it has instead is a confident, lived-in character: grand boulevards and shady squares, three of the world’s finest art museums within a short stroll of one another, a royal palace that looks over a deep valley, and a population that treats the street as an extension of the living room.

Spain’s capital sits almost exactly in the middle of the country, on a plateau about 650 metres above sea level. The light is clear, the sky is often a deep blue and the air is dry. Visitors who come for the Prado and the Reina Sofía soon discover the real attraction: the rhythm of the day. Coffee and a tostada in the morning, a caña and a small plate before a late lunch, an evening paseo, and a dinner that begins when many northern Europeans are already in bed.

SMART START TIP

Madrid rewards a flexible rhythm. Do the big sight in the morning, eat a late lunch, rest, and then come back out at dusk when the squares fill up. The city only really starts to hum around nine in the evening.
