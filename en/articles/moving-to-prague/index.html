Your Prague roadmap

The first month is easier when tasks are completed in the right order.

When Priority Why it matters
Before arrival Confirm the right to live and work for every person moving A job offer does not automatically settle a partner’s or a child’s status.
Before arrival Prepare originals, apostilles or legalisations and certified Czech translations Some documents are slow or expensive to replace after you land.
First days Sign a written lease and ask how your stay is reported to the authorities Many later steps ask for a confirmed Prague address.
First weeks Report your stay to the Foreign Police if your status requires it There are fixed deadlines, and they differ by nationality and route.
First weeks Employer registration, health insurance and a bank account Employees are registered by the employer, but dependants need their own check.
Month 1 Choose a GP, find school or childcare, buy a transport pass and set up digital access Availability is local. Do not assume a place is waiting.
Months 1–3 Czech, starting with pronunciation and the alphabet with its accents Letters, bills, schools and surgeries often work in Czech only.
Before the first winter Check the heating type, the insulation and the energy supplier Winter bills and short grey days are what newcomers notice most.
Early in the year Check kindergarten and school enrolment windows Places in popular districts are limited and the dates are fixed.

THE ESSENTIAL DISTINCTION

Where you live, whether you may stay and whether you may work are three separate questions. A lease or a reported address is not a residence permit and it is not permission to work.

The administrative essentials

A short list of the things almost every newcomer has to organise, and who handles each.

Task Who is involved Why it matters
Reporting your stay or registering your residence Foreign Police (Cizinecká policie), Ministry of the Interior, landlord It establishes your lawful stay and is asked for by many other offices.
Residence card or visa, if you need one Czech embassy, then Ministry of the Interior (Ministerstvo vnitra) It decides how long you may stay and under what conditions.
Employer registration Employer, Czech Social Security Administration (ČSSZ), health insurer It creates your pension and sickness-benefit record and your health cover.
Health insurance A health-insurance company, for example VZP, or a commercial insurer for non-employees Without valid cover you pay for care yourself, and permits may be refused.
Tax Financial Administration (Finanční správa) and your employer The employer withholds monthly tax. You may need an annual return in some cases.
Bank account A bank or fintech provider Salary, rent and utilities are paid by transfer or direct debit.
Mobile number A mobile operator or virtual operator It is used for banking codes, official logins and the transport app.

Reporting your stay

Czechia expects you to report your place of stay. The exact rule depends on who you are and what kind of stay you have. EU/EEA/Swiss citizens who stay for more than 30 days generally have to report their stay to the Foreign Police, and may apply for a certificate of temporary residence after three months. Non-EU residents with a long-term visa or residence permit have to keep the Foreign Police informed of changes of address. In many cases the accommodation provider, which includes a private landlord, has a duty to report a foreign tenant’s stay, but you remain responsible for checking that it was done. Ask your landlord to confirm in writing what they will do and when.

Czech citizens and permanent residents with a Czech ID are registered by their permanent address (trvalý pobyt), which is administered by the municipal district office. That is a different notion from where you actually live, and it appears in many forms.

THE ADDRESS QUESTION

Before you sign a lease, ask the landlord two things in writing: will they give you the documents you need to prove your address, and will they cooperate with the Foreign Police reporting? A landlord who refuses is a landlord to think twice about.

Numbers you will meet

  • Birth number (rodné číslo): a ten-digit identifier that is widely used in the Czech system and is also assigned to foreigners in many procedures.
  • Insured-person number (číslo pojištěnce): the number under which your health insurance is held. For many people it is the birth number.
  • Tax identification number (DIČ): used for tax matters, especially if you are self-employed.
  • Bank identity (Bank iD): a way to confirm your identity for online services through your bank login.
