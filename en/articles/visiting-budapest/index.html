When to go and what the weather means

Budapest has four real seasons, and each shows a different side of the city.

Season Typical character Good to know
Spring (March–May) Mild, fresh, blossom in the parks Rain showers are common; terraces reopen
Summer (June–August) Warm to hot, long evenings Heatwaves can be intense; baths and river life are at their best
Autumn (September–November) Golden light, grape harvest, culture season October is often settled and sunny; evenings turn cool
Winter (December–February) Cold, sometimes snowy, atmospheric Christmas markets, thermal baths in steam, short days

Month by month

  • Spring: a good time for walking, with the Budapest Spring Festival and the first terrace evenings.
  • Summer: festivals and open-air cinemas. Midday heat can make sightseeing tiring, so start early and use the baths and cool churches in the afternoon.
  • Autumn: the best compromise of weather, culture and crowds. Wine festivals and the start of the concert season fall here.
  • Winter: January and February can be grey and cold, but a steaming outdoor bath in the snow is one of the city’s best experiences.

Public holidays

Hungary has national holidays on 15 March, Easter Monday, 1 May, Whit Monday, 20 August (St Stephen’s Day), 23 October and Christmas. Many shops close on Sundays and holidays, and public transport may follow a different timetable. Check dates for your travel year on an official source.

SMART START TIP

If you can choose, aim for late April to early June or mid-September to mid-October. The light is beautiful, queues are manageable and a terrace dinner is still possible.

One day in Budapest

Castle Hill, the Danube bridges and a proper Hungarian dinner.

Morning

  1. Start on Castle Hill in Buda. Take the funicular or climb the steps from the Chain Bridge side. Visit Matthias Church and Fisherman’s Bastion early, before the tour groups arrive.
  2. Walk through the Castle District’s quiet streets towards the Buda Castle complex and its courtyards and terraces.
  3. Descend and cross the Széchenyi Chain Bridge on foot to Pest.

Afternoon

  1. Lunch near Vörösmarty tér or in the Great Market Hall: a bowl of goulash, a lángos or a plate of stuffed cabbage.
  2. Walk along the Pest embankment to the Hungarian Parliament and the Shoes on the Danube Bank memorial.
  3. Continue to St Stephen’s Basilica and its square.

Evening

  1. Stroll along Andrássy Avenue to the Opera House as the lights come on.
  2. Dinner in a traditional restaurant or a modern Hungarian bistro.
  3. Finish with a glass of wine or pálinka in a wine bar or a ruin bar in the Jewish Quarter.

SMART START TIP

If you have one extra hour, see the Parliament from the water on an evening river cruise. If you have an extra morning, add a bath such as Széchenyi in the City Park.
