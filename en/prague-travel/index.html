Inside the opening chapter

Prague (Praha) is one of Europe’s best-preserved capitals. Its medieval, Renaissance, Baroque and Art Nouveau streets came through the twentieth century almost intact, so the skyline you see from Charles Bridge is, in outline, the one that travellers admired five hundred years ago. A castle the size of a small town crowns the hill on the left bank; an astronomical clock chimes on the right; and between them the Vltava curves past islands, weirs, rowing boats and riverside bars.

The city is also far more than its postcard. Behind the crowds of the Old Town lie leafy hillsides, cubist façades, tram-rattled neighbourhoods, concert halls where Dvořák and Smetana were played, and pubs where the beer is poured as carefully as wine. Czech food is hearty, Czech humour is dry and Czech hospitality, once you step off the tourist trail, is quietly generous.

SMART START TIP

Prague is best explored in layers. Do the famous sights early in the morning or after the day-trippers leave, and keep the middle of the day for a museum, a café or a tram ride across town. The best moments are often found between the landmarks: a courtyard passage, a viewpoint, a wine bar in a vaulted cellar.
