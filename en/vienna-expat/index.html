Inside the opening chapter

Vienna regularly appears at or near the top of international quality-of-life rankings. It is praised for public transport, healthcare, education, stability, green spaces and cultural life. It held first place in the Economist Intelligence Unit’s liveability ranking in 2018, 2019, 2022, 2023 and 2024; in 2026 it again placed second, behind Copenhagen.

That sounds impressive. But what does “liveable” mean when you have just arrived with several suitcases, a new employment contract and a family trying to understand where it belongs?

FROM MORE THAN 15 YEARS IN VIENNA

Vienna’s famous quality of life is real—but it does not arrive automatically with your new address. You experience it gradually: when the tram becomes familiar, when your child begins to understand German, and when an official letter that once looked frightening becomes something you know how to handle.
