Inside the opening chapter

Paris is the capital of France and the heart of the Île-de-France region, a global centre of business, fashion, research, culture and gastronomy. The city itself is compact, divided into twenty arrondissements, each with its own mairie and its own character. Around it, the suburbs and the wider region are where many people actually live, work and send their children to school. The big decisions are national. Many of the daily ones are local.

That sounds enchanting. But what does “a great place to live” mean when you have just landed with several suitcases, a new employment contract and a family trying to work out where it belongs?

THE POSTCARD AND THE TUESDAY

Paris’s quality of life is real, but it does not arrive with your new address. It builds gradually: when the Métro map becomes familiar, when the boulangerie knows your order, and when a letter from the *impôts* or the *CAF* stops looking alarming.
